note_draft: "Luxury arrives at the battlefield in a chariot, scattering violets and rose-petals at the Christian ranks, and very nearly wins — until Sobriety lifts the cross before the horses' eyes and the whole gaudy equipage goes over. Prudentius composed the Psychomachia around 400, and with it the personified Virtues and Vices that medieval Europe carved into a thousand porches and cathedral fronts. The allegory is stranger, and a great deal bloodier, than its reputation. This is the anonymous 1743 English version, which turns the Latin hexameters into heroic couplets and fights all seven combats at full length."
