## What transfers

The author page reuses Cranach's 1520 portrait from the Reformation context collection. A short interpretive passage explains this dated image of Luther, with links to the museum and era page. Four actual published Luther records populate the existing cover components. The 15-text total includes separately catalogued sections, not 15 distinct complete works. Reading estimates use stored word counts at 200 words/minute, rounded up to five-minute increments.

The reader uses the published Ten Commandments section of the Small Catechism (accession 594), showing the first three commandments as a clearly labelled excerpt. Its recorded translation is Robert E. Smith's 1994 Project Wittenberg text, with an explicit public-domain dedication recorded in the catalogue. The whole Small Catechism record (218) is a different catalogue entry and translation; these were not conflated.

An optional panel reuses Amman's [1568] printing image with a text-specific invitation to consider household teaching. The caption identifies it as later period context, not Luther's own printer. The passage retains identical position and width between the two states. Author-to-reader and open/close navigation reactions are wired in Figma; era and museum references are links.

## Content and design boundaries

See `reformation-era-illustrated-content-draft.md` for full image sources, rights evidence and reuse rules. Biography and contextual introductions are editorial mockup copy, not database changes. Book titles may be shortened for covers. No publication flags, production code or catalogue content changed.

These are editable desktop compositions, checked through rendered screenshots and layer/font inspection. Images are discrete historical assets, not flattened page screenshots. The reader displays an excerpt, not the entire text. Mobile layout, keyboard focus, screen-reader behavior, and production interactions remain implementation work; a production panel should return focus to its trigger on close and respect the user's reading position.
