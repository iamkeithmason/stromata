## Why each was chosen

- **homily-21 → Basil, Address to Young Men.** Both are fourth-century Fathers on raising the young, from the parent's and the pupil's side. The retired Vainglory text's pick was this same Basil address, so this keeps that path open for the readers the redirect now sends to Homily 21. Alternate: Clement's *Paedagogus* I, on Christ as the tutor of children.
- **Tyndale → Hus, Christ the Only Head of the Church.** *The Practice of Prelates* argues that prelates have taken temporal power Christ never gave; Hus's chapter argues from scripture that Christ alone heads the Church. It is also a lander in its own right (2 entries). Alternate: Wycliffe on curates who chase worldly goods, the English root of Tyndale's complaint.
- **Zwingli → Luther, Babylonian Captivity.** Chapter 4 of the *Exposition* rejects any bodily eating of Christ in the Supper, the point on which Zwingli and Luther broke at Marburg. *Babylonian Captivity* (1520) is Luther's fullest treatment of the sacraments; the *Exposition* was written in July 1531. Alternate: Luther's *Small Catechism*, shorter, and has his plain statement on the Sacrament of the Altar.
- **Wolf → Institutes of Polity.** Same author, but a standalone text, so not excluded. The Sermon is the lament, the *Institutes* the remedy; the Nov 9 digest saw both land in the same week. Alternate: Alfred's Preface, a king's lament for England's lost learning a century earlier, which pairs naturally with the Wolf.
- **Basil, On the Holy Spirit → Gregory Nazianzen, Oration 31.** Basil famously avoided calling the Spirit "God" in so many words, arguing instead from the doxology ("with the Holy Spirit"); Gregory's oration, a few years after Basil's death, does say it ("if He is God"). The reason is a fact about the two texts, not an interpretation. Alternate: the Athanasian Creed, where the argument ends up.
- **Jubaianus → Seventh Council of Carthage.** The council's record opens with the reading of Jubaianus's letter and Cyprian's reply, this one. Same author, but not a collection, so permitted. Note: the council's stored summary dates it to 258; the council met in 256 (Cyprian died in 258). Worth correcting separately. Alternate: Tertullian, *On Baptism*, whose ch. 15 already denied heretics' baptism.
- **Fidus → Tertullian, On Baptism.** Ch. 18 of *On Baptism* counsels delay "principally… in the case of little children"; Cyprian's council decided against waiting even eight days. Jerome reports Cyprian calling Tertullian "the master", which supports "the teacher he most admired". Alternate: the Didache, the oldest instructions for baptism.
- **Ignatius, Ephesians → Polycarp, Philippians.** Polycarp's ch. 13 is about sending on Ignatius's letters; ch. 9 names "the blessed Ignatius". The Smyrna stop is Ignatius's own (he wrote Ephesians from Smyrna). Alternate: *The Martyrdom of Polycarp*, the same church's account of its bishop's death.
- **Psychomachia → Evagrius, Practical Life.** Evagrius's ch. 6 lists the eight thoughts that became the Western vices Prudentius personifies. Evagrius died c. 399; the *Psychomachia* is c. 405, so "a generation earlier" is loose: I'd accept "a few years earlier" if Keith prefers precision. The pick chains on to Cassian, since Evagrius already points there. Alternate: Cassian, Conference 5, on the eight principal faults.
- **Alfred → Gregory, Pastoral Rule Part I.** The Preface introduces Alfred's translation of exactly this book, sent to each bishop with an *æstel*. Part I is Gregory on who should and shouldn't seek pastoral office. Alternate: Sermon of the Wolf, the reverse of the Wolf's pairing.
