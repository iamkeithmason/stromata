# Published provenance coverage — 21 Nov 2026

Card: *Published provenance coverage backfill*. Read-only: nothing was written. The figures come from
Supabase (`texts`, `collections`, `prospects`) and the staged output files, and were measured on 21 Nov 2026
against the **565 published texts**. Egress was negligible: metadata columns only, no `body`.

## Coverage, field by field

| Field | Published texts with it | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| `source_attribution` (the edition, as a sentence) | 562 (99.5%) | 3: `a-sermon-of-the-paschal-lamb`, `the-apology-of-al-kindy-…`, `of-good-marriage` |
| `source_url` (where the text was taken from) | 385 (68%) | **180** |
| `provenance_notes` | 519 (92%) | 46 |
| `translation_notes` | 505 (89%) | 60 |
| `ocr_quality` | 520 (92%) | 45 |

The edition is almost always recorded. The gap is the **structured `source_url`**: 180 texts, 32% of the
published corpus, have none.

## A reliable path to higher coverage

1. **Write the 27 above** (one small script or SQL, verifying each URL answers before it is stored). That
   takes `source_url` coverage from 68% to 73%. Uncertain data stays out: nothing is inferred.
2. **Look up the 48 in D and E**: from each work's index page, match each book's own page by its title, fetch
   it, and check the title in the first lines before writing. That reaches 81%. The same subagent fan-out as a
   Drive batch suits it, one work per agent.
3. **Leave F as "no web source"** unless Keith wants a public copy found. Record it plainly instead of leaving
   the field blank, so the gap reads as known, not missing.
4. **G and H are the research queue** (47 texts, mostly ANF/NPNF, whose edition is already named).

Nothing here changes what a reader sees: `source_url` feeds the provenance panel and future corpus links, and
the human rights check stays authoritative.
